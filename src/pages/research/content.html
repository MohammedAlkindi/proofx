GB-001 · Goldbach partition selection

Finding small observed-to-expected partition ratios

Does ProofX's structural candidate selection recover more low-ratio integers than uniform sampling or an ascending scan, given the same evaluation budget?

Domain

Metric
Recall of the bottom 5% of ratios

For each integer, G(n) counts unordered prime pairs, including equal primes. The ratio is G(n) / H(n), where H(n) is the leading unordered Hardy–Littlewood approximation. An exhaustive reference defines the lowest 5%, including boundary ties. Policies select candidates without reading that reference.

Interpretation limits

In this published run, directed selection finds 19, 12, and 19 low-ratio inputs across the three seeds; uniform sampling finds 8, 4, and 4. The ascending control finds 26 in each row. Directed selection therefore does not beat both baselines here. The ascending control concentrates on small integers, where the approximation has the largest finite-size error; this result motivates comparisons within held-out size intervals.

The approximation has finite-size error, especially for small integers. Finding low ratios measures alignment with this chosen heuristic; it does not measure progress toward a counterexample. The policies visit different size distributions. Repeating this protocol on prespecified held-out intervals is necessary before arguing that a pattern generalizes.

The verifier independently checks every exact count and witness, hashes, domain, budget, and summary. It does not authenticate authorship or certify timings. Rerun the recorded policies and compare ledger hashes to check policy provenance.
